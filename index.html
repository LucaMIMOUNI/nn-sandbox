BatchNorm, and what it averages over

Every normalisation layer does the same arithmetic — subtract a mean, divide by a standard deviation, then rescale with γ and β. The only thing that changes between BatchNorm, LayerNorm, InstanceNorm and GroupNorm is which numbers get pooled into one mean. Hover any cell: everything outside its pooling group is greyed out, and the maths is written underneath. At the bottom, the problem it was invented for — the input distribution moving under a layer while it trains.

Backprop, one derivative at a time

One neuron fitted to a handful of points. The forward pass runs left to right along the top — each station carries its own equation with this point's numbers already substituted in, and is filled with the colour of the number it holds: strong when it is large, faded as it approaches zero. The backward pass runs right to left directly underneath, station for station, so every gradient sits below the value it came from. Inside the neuron and the loss, the derivative being multiplied is drawn as a tangent line — flat means the gradient dies there. Drag a wire to change its weight, or press play and watch the curve settle onto the points as the error bars shrink.

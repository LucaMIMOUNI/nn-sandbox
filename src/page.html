BatchNorm, and what it averages over

Every normalisation layer does the same arithmetic — subtract a mean, divide by a standard deviation, then rescale with γ and β. The only thing that changes between BatchNorm, LayerNorm, InstanceNorm and GroupNorm is which numbers get pooled into one mean. Hover any cell: everything outside its pooling group is greyed out, and the maths is written underneath. At the bottom, the problem it was invented for — the input distribution moving under a layer while it trains.

Backprop, one derivative at a time

One neuron fitted to a handful of points, in seven steps. The forward pass runs left to right along the top; the backward pass runs right to left directly underneath, one multiplication per step, so every gradient sits below the value it came from. Every number is tinted by its own size — strong when it is large, faded as it approaches zero — and each derivative being multiplied is drawn as a tangent line, flat meaning the gradient dies there. Drag a wire to change its weight, or press play and watch the fit on the right settle onto the points: a dot is where a point should be, the line is the neuron, and the bar between them is that point's error.

negative → zero → positive, and how strongly gradients, and the tangents they are read off in the maths: a value, a parameter, a gradient — every term carries the colour its number has on the canvas
